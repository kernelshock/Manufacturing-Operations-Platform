## 3. Evidence grades

- `VERIFIED`: independently corroborated source evidence, **not automatically statistically sufficient**. Future variance estimates must check sample size, date range and representativeness.
- `PARTIAL`: some verified events exist, but original PO / receipts / claims / promise history may be missing. Recommendation is PROVISIONAL unless a selected policy demands verified completeness.
- `UNKNOWN`: essential facts absent; model blocks the recommendation instead of using zero or assumed reliability.

The pure readiness model returns `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, `PROVISIONAL` or `BLOCKED`, with explicit reason codes and no purchase-order effect. These describe *evidence readiness*, not a commitment to supply or Quality release. Even READY_FOR_REVIEW requires authorized human decision.

Current readiness blockers: physical UNKNOWN, Quality-usable UNKNOWN, unverifiable business calendar, supplier lead time UNKNOWN, source coverage UNKNOWN, or a VARIABILITY model with PARTIAL/UNKNOWN supplier history. Plant mismatches fail with a policy error.

No double counting of both per-seat and per-product historic values representing the same consumption.

## 5. Calculations reserved for later gates

- `P.P. = E[consumption during replenishment lead time] + safety stock`.
- Baseline percentage policy candidate: `SS = safety_percentage × expected lead-time demand`. This is only a scenario definition and must be approved before activation, not a measured variability estimate.
- A future variability policy may use demand and lead-time dispersion, subject to verified sample size and compatible independent units; do not hardcode a statistical model now.
- `C.R.`: FIXED uses a validated positive quantity; CALCULATED later considers target stock, confirmed requirements, MOQ, pack multiple and verified in-transit deliveries, never conflating invoice/PO with receipt.

All quantities remain exact `Decimal` and canonical UoM. Present unknown quantities as UNKNOWN, not numeric 0. Round only for explicit final presentation or supplier pack multiples with a documented rule.

## 6. Synthetic acceptance cases

| Scenario | Contract result |
| --- | --- |
| Known physical zero and known Quality-usable zero | READY_FOR_REVIEW if all other facts are verified; calculated quantity remains zero, never UNKNOWN |
| Uninitialized location, physical UNKNOWN | BLOCKED, no usable quantity |
| Quality status UNKNOWN even with known physical stock | BLOCKED |
| Partial Prolowin transcription with original promises uncertain | PROVISIONAL under simple policy; variability method BLOCKED |
| Supplier delay source UNKNOWN | BLOCKED; do not assume no delay |
| Unverified business calendar | BLOCKED |
| Shared material, two product families, two taquillas | Source paths retained; no cross-plant aggregation |
| Plant mismatch between policy and facts | Error, not pooled recommendation |
| Active holds > physical or Quality usable > physical | Error |
| Fixed CR = 0 or missing | Error |
| Automatic PO flag requested | Error |
| Verified supplier receipt without operational physical stock | Physical remains UNKNOWN; BLOCKED |

## 7. Decisions deferred to relevant owners (do not invent)

1. Exact calendars for FAIC holidays, workdays and individual supplier working days; whether the clock begins at PO issue or supplier acknowledgement.
2. Statistical minimum sample size and service-level target per supplier/article; distinguish policy assumptions from evidence.
3. Source of Quality usability/inspection acceptance and per-lot balances.
4. Valid minimum order quantity, packaging, substitute supplier qualification and replenishment constraints from verified supplier master data.
5. Actual operational permissions for planners, purchasing, Quality, management report readers and final approval; enforce by IAM in G7.6.
6. Historical lookback period and forecast horizon per material category.
7. Thresholds for production-risk escalation and emergency alternate supplier review.

Until resolved, these fields are explicit configuration or BLOCKED/PROVISIONAL, never embedded guesses.
